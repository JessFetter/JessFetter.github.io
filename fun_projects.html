Fun / Random Projects

A few side projects and creative builds outside of the main engineering coursework—small, hands-on, and meant to keep learning fun.

This chess set CAD was completed during high school and was my first real experience working with CAD in AutoCAD. It was a fun project that let me explore design and modeling in a creative way before I came to Purdue.

This gingerbread house was built over my Christmas break during senior year of high school. I fully CADed the design to generate the cut plans, then assembled the structure over two weeks, turning a festive idea into a carefully planned, hands-on build that combined creativity, precision, and problem-solving.

I created this simple telegraph for a high school social studies project. It uses batteries and wires to power an electromagnet, making it a working telegraph that combined engineering with a historical concept in a fun and hands-on way.

This metal rose was made at Bechtel, Purdue’s makerspace, and although it was a smaller project, it was a fun one to make. It allowed me to focus on detail, craftsmanship, and the satisfaction of creating something with my hands.

This was one of my first real explorations in CAD, done during high school as a way to learn how to build shapes in 3D and start understanding form, proportion, and design decisions.

This tulip model was another fun CAD project from the same high school design work, and it gave me a chance to explore more organic, artistic shapes while continuing to build my skills in AutoCAD.

I built this trebuchet during my freshman year of high school for my older sister’s physics competition. She won that year, and for the next two years I was able to compete and win as well, making this trebuchet the four-year reigning champion for the pumpkin launch competition. When my other sister competed, she also won, so the trebuchet became a true family legacy and a four-year title holder.

More small creative experiments and hobby work will be added here over time.
